What the flows would mean

Examples that separate the working V1 watcher from future Station V2 concepts.

Every name, address, transaction, amount, and key in this page is intentionally absent or fictional. These are teaching scenarios, not commands, a service offer, financial advice, or a production workflow.

1. Viewer recovery SHIPPED & VERIFIED

A viewer receives a share for fictional “Signal Garden.” Instead of trusting the name alone, they select Sepolia, paste the exact Station deployment transaction into Archive, and choose the publisher-specific result. When the beacon endpoint cannot serve a sidecar, they try a configured fallback endpoint and only play a payload after the watcher’s verification succeeds. The result is a verified past segment—not proof that Signal Garden is live now.

2. Local-file publisher EXPERIMENTAL

A creator prepares a fictional short film on a local machine. Before any signing, they choose Sepolia, a dedicated low-value wallet, a known Station, and an independent maximum exposure. They segment locally, inspect the resulting manifest, and stop when a fee or wallet check exceeds their limit. Closing a UI or losing the machine interrupts local work; it does not turn the system into an autonomous broadcaster.

3. Scheduled replay PLANNED

A future Station could assign an already committed fictional asset to a disclosed block-range reservation. At that range, clients would play the referenced asset as scheduled replay. Because the mode is an assignment, it is different from a live publisher and from a standing fallback. The current V1 watcher has no such schedule adapter or UI.

4. Standing fallback EXPERIMENTAL / PLANNED

Imagine a Station with no active valid reservation and a previously committed catalog of fictional assets. A future compatible client would deterministically try bounded candidates and could choose one without sending a playback-time transaction. The UI must say fallback/replay, not live. Provider failure may lead to a different observed result, but it must not change the proposed canonical candidate order.

5. `.gwei` recovery PLANNED

A fictional name such as signal-garden.gwei appears stale or conflicting. A safe client would show the resolution state and fall back to the direct Station/channel address and generation evidence; it would not move history or funds simply because a display name changed. Current users should use exact V1 Station and publisher identity because no public watcher resolver exists.

6. Airtime allocation PLANNED

A future Station offers a block lot through a disclosed policy. A fictional applicant sees the policy version, eligibility rules, commitment window, cancellation/release rules, and the distinction between an airtime bid and transmission costs. They do not treat a client forecast as a winning bid, an inclusion guarantee, or a claim on Station funds.

7. Fee spike EXPERIMENTAL

A planner’s fictional snapshot has base, guarded, and stress scenarios. A later blob-fee increase exceeds the operator’s maximum exposure. The safe action is to stop new publication and follow the declared future policy—such as reduced density, release, a higher user contribution, or fallback—after public policy verification. The snapshot itself cannot authorize reimbursement or make an unavailable transaction affordable.

8. Claim and recovery PLANNED / OPEN

A future publisher believes a policy-bound action created a reimbursement entitlement. They would need a released contract, exact Station/reservation context, confirmation/finality state, one-time action identity, and a pull-based claim path. Until those are public and reviewed, there is no claimable reimbursement, protected reserve, or recovery instruction to execute.

Reading the examples safely

  • Use direct V1 evidence for anything you do today.
  • Use experimental scenarios to understand data boundaries, not to configure a production integration.
  • Treat planned/open scenarios as questions for the eventual public specification and reviews.
  • Never substitute an example for independent verification of the chain, contract, endpoint, wallet, or policy.
